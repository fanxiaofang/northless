# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Gap Cockpit serves self-directed explorers, makers, and knowledge workers who do not have a reliable external cadence. They use it as a private, local-first cockpit to capture loose thoughts, keep several long-running tracks warm, and return to meaningful work without turning life into a scoreboard.

## Product Purpose

The product records what actually happened, keeps active tracks visible, and suggests a clear next move. Success means the user can regain orientation quickly, preserve momentum across gaps, and capture an idea without first organizing it.

## Brand Personality

Calm, tactile, and quietly precise. The interface should feel like a well-used instrument panel: warm graphite, aged metal, restrained optical depth, and language that lowers pressure rather than manufacturing urgency.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards made from interchangeable floating cards.
- Gamified productivity systems that create streak, clearing, or red-yellow-green performance anxiety.
- Ornamental steampunk that sacrifices legibility or familiar controls for costume.
- Heavy glass panels, exaggerated glow, and thick elevation that make every region compete for attention.

## Design Principles

- Make capture lighter than analysis: input surfaces invite a quick deposit, while analytical surfaces may carry more optical weight.
- Share surface physics, not identical containers: related panels use the same edge, light, and material vocabulary at different strengths.
- Record reality without judgment: history communicates movement and continuity, not performance grading.
- Keep hierarchy earned: a surface gains depth only when its task benefits from stronger containment.
- Preserve an open workspace: empty and secondary regions should breathe instead of becoming nested cards.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for readable text and interactive controls. Preserve visible keyboard focus, complete keyboard operation for capture and navigation, semantic status announcements, adequate non-color cues, and reduced-motion behavior whenever motion is introduced.
